Estimating π with darts

Throw darts uniformly at random at the unit square. The quarter circle has area π/4 and the square has area 1, so the fraction of darts landing inside is close to π/4 — and four times that fraction is an estimate of π.

What to notice

  • With 100 darts the estimate is often off by 0.1 or more. With 100,000 it is usually right to two decimal places.
  • The typical error shrinks like 1.64/√n: every extra correct digit costs a hundred times as many darts. That slow but dependable √n is the price, and the strength, of Monte Carlo methods.
  • Nothing here is special to circles: the same idea estimates areas and averages in hundreds of dimensions, where no grid of points could ever work.
