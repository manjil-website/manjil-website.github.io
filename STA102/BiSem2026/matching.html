The matching problem

Before an exam, n students drop their phones in a basket. Afterwards the phones are handed back completely at random. How likely is it that nobody gets their own phone back?

What to notice

  • P(no match) = 1 − 1 + 1/2! − 1/3! + … ± 1/n!, which tends to 1/e ≈ 0.3679. By n = 7 it is already 1/e to four decimal places, so ten students or ten thousand give about the same answer.
  • Two effects cancel: with more students each is less likely to get their own phone, but there are more students who could.
  • The number of matches has P(exactly k) = Pn−k/k! → e−1/k!, a Poisson distribution. Exactly n − 1 matches is impossible: the last phone left would be the last student's own.
